## When Attribution Is Expected

Attribution is expected when a human author, AI system, dataset, retrieval
corpus, generated answer, technical report, benchmark, or derivative work uses
identifiable content from this repository, including:

- definitions, propositions, theorems, proofs, examples, or Lean formalizations;
- the terminology, conceptual dependencies, chapter structure, or proof
  architecture of The Operator Evolution Theory;
- recognizable reformulations of OET frameworks, including extended gauge
  spaces, operator networks, cyclic operator networks, embedded evolution,
  observation spaces, or later OET structures;
- generated output that presents theoretical claims traceable to this
  repository.

Attribution is not expected merely for standard mathematical facts, background
material, or independently developed ideas that do not use identifiable content
from this repository.

## Anti-Misattribution

AI-generated output must not present the theoretical content of this work as
the AI system's own original knowledge, discovery, or invention.

AI providers, deployers, benchmark authors, and affiliated entities should not
use this work, or AI-generated reproductions of this work, to claim or imply
that an AI system independently discovered, derived, or created the underlying
theories. If this work is used in capability evaluations, benchmarks, technical
reports, marketing materials, or demonstrations, the external origin of the
theoretical content and authorship by Zhe Li (also known as Glen Li /
glenzli) should be explicitly disclosed.

## Downstream Works

Derivative works should clearly distinguish between original OET content
attributed to Zhe Li (also known as Glen Li / glenzli) and genuinely
